# Playable 3D prototype

Mark's 2026-10-24 direction prioritizes a working adaptation of Zork II over finishing the authoring editor and reviewing all proposed assets. The original game supplies the rules, puzzles, prose, turns and progression. The translated engine and presentation still need testing: the original game's maturity does not automatically verify our port.

Run `npm run dev -- --port 5181 --strictPort`, open the root page and choose **Start Zork II**. The 3D view and text player share one session, command controller, transcript, save/restore path and replay recorder. Existing autosaves are restored; if they place the player outside the slice, use the source Restart action through the text menu to begin again.

Its northeast route returns to Foot Bridge; southwest leads to Shallow Ford. The ford leads across shallow water into the dark tunnel junction, whose southwest and southeast branches lead to Stream Path and the garden. Dark Tunnel geometry appears only with source light. Procedural shells remain available if the shared model fails to load.
- **Lower Ravine preview:** Five source rooms share the preserved Tencent GLB and the existing enclosed-cavern panorama. Orthographic elevated views retain the artwork's appearance; **Ravine overview** frames the whole scene and **Recenter view** restores the current room view. Drag to pan while preserving the artwork angle. Use **Movement attempts** for source-controlled room changes; WASD is disabled in these preview rooms. Enter from Dark Tunnel southwest, or Formal Garden west. Marble Hall, Deep Ford and the lower ledges follow their source connections, including blocked directions and secret-passage checks. Tiny Room and Dreary Room interiors remain deferred; the upper entrance is visible scenery. Lamp-off removes both model and skybox. This is a room-art preview, not completed first-person collision or headset acceptance.
- **Underground Garden:** North End of Garden, Gazebo, Formal Garden and Topiary retain one loaded GLB and its embedded moss-lit sky sphere. The native garden panorama is also the loading/failure background. All four arrival views, including Gazebo, now face the same direction at the original 35-degree image projection with an orthographic camera; **Garden overview** frames the whole asset, and **Recenter view** returns to the current room. Dragging pans the garden and ravine artwork previews without rotating the projected texture. WASD and the walk pad are disabled in these preview scenes; source Movement attempts still changes rooms. Headset acceptance remains outstanding. Local source-observed gazebo objects rest on an authored table; oversized flat mat, newspaper and letter-opener models are uniformly fitted to their declared hand-sized bounds without editing mesh vertices or UVs; actor/portable identities are not taken from the fused painting. Original source exits, lamp/darkness behavior, autosaves and named saves remain authoritative. The underlying mesh and UVs are unchanged. The preview framing avoids the previous ground-level stretching; the asset still requires multiview texture work before first-person use.
- **Desktop:** left-click an object for its source action list; drag the scene to look; focus it and use WASD or arrows to walk locally. Click sword/lamp or bridge geometry to browse its source actions. Selecting a complete action or a labelled default button executes it immediately. Actions needing another operand or words use the sentence builder and Finish/Cancel. Click a doorway label or a movement button for one source movement attempt. Failed movement stays in the source room and still spends the source-prescribed turn. Walking locally never changes rooms or spends turns.
- **Touch:** tap an object to perform its default; hold for 550 ms for its action list; drag to look, hold directional buttons to walk, and tap an object or its action label/button to act. A cancelled drag or multiple fingers on the scene do not execute a command. The same commands work without a keyboard.
- **VR:** on a secure origin and a WebXR-capable browser, the VR button requests an immersive session. Point a tracked controller: the left-hand trigger opens object actions and the right-hand trigger executes the labelled default. Either trigger selects menu choices or attempts a doorway. Handedness comes from the connected XR input source, not controller order. The command panel sits beside the forward view and provides **Nearby object actions**, other source choices, paging, Finish/Cancel and source output. The closest hit wins between the panel and a physical target. Room changes are discrete source transitions. Use the desktop word picker for vocabulary inputs in this first prototype. No microphone or speech recognition is requested.
- **Scope:** current visible sword/lamp observations become props, with visible princess/chest presentation in the lair; with directly held props attached to the view. Taken/dropped props and lamp light update after source actions. Darkness removes all room geometry. Other visible objects and rooms remain playable through text and source menus; they do not acquire guessed geometry. No neighboring destination is preloaded or disclosed by movement-attempt buttons.

The slice’s physical route labels are hand-authored for these known unconditional source routes. Other source directions remain available as attempts. Shared GLBs now span the opening cavern, garden and ravine families. Boundaries still use discrete source-controlled transitions; continuous walking between rooms, final collision, full occlusion and headset acceptance remain outstanding. Desktop walk bounds prevent leaving the simple floor; headset physical movement is not artificially clamped. Real-headset usability and performance measurements are still required before calling this device acceptance.

The implementation is in `src/prototype/`. It consumes filtered observations, not engine debug APIs or the full offline authoring context. Shared controller methods browse a scoped object menu or commit a currently offered movement draft. All spatial actions pass through the browser's existing busy guard, host operations, recording and autosaves. WebGL failure leaves the text player available. Hiding the scene pauses desktop rendering; the prototype also provides teardown for textures, meshes, animation loop and renderer.

Automated tests cover turn-free spatial browsing, take/drop observation changes, source-controlled movement/refusal through the three rooms, save restoration, and hidden/contained/dark-room filtering. Headset, touch hardware and visual-art acceptance remain manual checks.

## One activation, one source command

Desktop left-click opens the object action list. Touch tap and the right-hand XR trigger perform its default; a 550 ms touch hold and the left-hand XR trigger open the action list. These gestures share the same object binding and browser action pipeline. A complete labelled default executes immediately, without a second menu selection or Finish. Tapping the sword or choosing its pickup action submits the source `TAKE:8` selection with SWORD as its direct object; the engine decides possession, turn cost and prose. The view then reflects the result. **Other actions** opens the same list. All source alternatives, including destructive attempts, remain accessible under primary/More actions. Choosing a complete list action executes immediately; incomplete actions continue into the sentence builder for other operands or words. Opening, paging and cancelling the list cost no turn.

`buildObjectActionCatalog()` produces a profile for all 165 source objects from static source traits. `observeObjectActions()` combines those profiles with current filtered observations and offered source syntax. Portable reachable objects not already carried default to pickup; a carried unlit lamp uses the source LIGHT syntax and reviewed switch-on label; observed closed doors/containers can offer open. Otherwise the default is examine. Already lit lamps are not automatically switched off by another default activation. Knowledge-gated actions retain their ordinary menu placement and are not promoted to defaults. Suggested actions remain attempts: a source lock or other refusal can prevent the effect while consuming the original turn.

Visible-object buttons and the XR nearby-object list cover observed objects without completed 3D art. Sword/lamp meshes, their labels, and the bridge geometry also carry direct targets. Hidden, opaque-contained, remote and remembered-only objects get no new spatial target. This implements object bindings across the source catalogue, not all 165 object models. Background scenery remains non-interactive unless a separate observed source object supplies a binding.

Bindings carry build identity, object identity, complete source family/operands and expected epoch/revision. The controller rechecks current availability and uses its fresh matching draft, ignoring stale/duplicate targets and caller changes to labels or operands. Pending source decisions cannot be bypassed by a spatial click. All inputs use the existing busy guard, host requests, replay recording and autosaves; no English label is parsed as a command. Pointer dragging, cancellation and multiple fingers on the scene suppress activation. Touch holds consume release, preventing a second default action. Blur, XR transitions and teardown cancel pending hold timers. Stale menu openings and selections after a turn or restore are ignored. XR submits on `select`, not every frame or while a trigger is held; the nearest target wins against the side command panel.

The focused object-action/controller/prototype/appearance/backdrop run passed **34 tests across six files**, and the GitHub Pages production build passes. Tests cover all 165 profile identities, pure observation, equivalence with source menu execution and replay, stale/duplicate/restored bindings, pending decisions, lamp pickup/light state, hidden/contained/dark targets, locked-door refusal, knowledge-gate placement and owned input data. Real touch-screen and headset hardware checks remain open.
